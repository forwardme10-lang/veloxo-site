How to train for your first 100 km ride

8 weeks · 3 to 4 rides a week · for riders who can already ride 40 to 50 km

A first 100 km ride, often called a century, is a big day but a very reachable one. You don't need to ride every day. You need one long ride a week that slowly gets longer, a couple of shorter rides around it, and enough rest to recover.

The 8-week plan

Ride three or four times a week. The long ride is usually at the weekend; the others are shorter rides in the week.

WeekLong rideIn the week
150 km, easyTwo rides of 45 to 60 min, easy
260 kmOne easy ride, one with 4 × 4 min a bit harder
370 kmOne easy ride, one with 4 × 5 min harder
455 km, easyTwo short easy rides. This is a lighter week so your body can catch up
580 kmOne easy ride, one with 3 × 8 min harder
690 kmOne easy ride, one with 3 × 10 min harder
765 kmOne easy ride, one short ride with a few 1 min efforts
8Your 100 kmTwo short easy rides early in the week, then rest the day before

Easy means you can hold a conversation. Harder means you can only say a few words at a time. Rest well between the efforts.

Missed a week? Don't try to catch up. Repeat the last week you finished and carry on from there.

Pacing on the day

  • Start easier than you think you should. The first hour should feel almost too easy.
  • Keep a steady effort on climbs instead of a steady speed. Shift down early.
  • Ride with a group if you can. Sitting behind other riders saves a lot of energy, but keep a safe gap.
  • Split the ride in your head: four rides of 25 km feel much smaller than one of 100.

What to eat and drink

  • Before: a normal breakfast with carbohydrates two to three hours before the start, like oats, idli, bread or rice.
  • On the bike: start eating in the first hour and keep going every 20 to 30 minutes. Aim for 30 to 60 g of carbohydrate an hour: a banana, a bar, dates or a gel.
  • Drink: about one bottle (500 to 750 ml) an hour, more when it's hot. Add electrolytes on hot days.
  • Practise this on your long training rides. Nothing new on the day.

Bike and kit

  • Get the bike checked a week before, not the day before.
  • Carry a spare tube, tyre levers, a pump or CO₂, and a little money.
  • Padded shorts, a helmet and sun cream. Wear kit you've already ridden long in.

The week of the ride

Ride less, not more. A couple of short easy rides keep your legs fresh. Sleep well, drink water through the day before, and check the route, start time and aid stations.
